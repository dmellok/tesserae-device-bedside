Before you start

Printed parts: frame plate, base tray and plinth in black; front bezel, rear shell and access cover in beige. Which way up to print each one is on the printing page.

Tools: hex keys for M2, M2.5, M3 and M4 socket cap screws, a small spanner or pliers for the encoder, switch and speaker nuts, thin double-sided tape for the battery, and a soft cloth to lay the screen on.

Every screw threads straight into the plastic. Stop turning as soon as the head seats: a stripped hole will not hold again. Also needed: four self-adhesive rubber feet, about 12 mm across and 2 mm thick, and single-sided EVA foam tape, 2 mm thick and 5 mm wide, to seat the panel.

Flashing the Pico

Flash the Pico before it goes into the case: it sits in its adapter facing down, and once the case is together its USB-C port cannot be reached, even with the access cover off. After that the panel updates itself over WiFi, so a cable is only needed again to recover a board that will not boot.

Disassembly

The assembly in reverse. Switch the device off and unplug USB-C first. The rubber feet stay on: every screw under the base is reachable between them. To reach the battery, speaker or USB-C inlet, stop after the plinth.
