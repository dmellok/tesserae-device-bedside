Amp and battery wiring to the Pi header

The MAX98357A amplifier and the PowerBoost 1000C both connect to the 40-pin Pi header on the GPIO splitter. Every wire below names the header pin, the Raspberry Pi BCM name printed on the splitter, and the Pico GPIO it reaches through the Pico-to-Pi adapter. These are the pins the firmware already uses.

The soldered board changes some of this. On the power and amp board every ground is one piece and the amp takes its 5 V from the board, so header pins 4, 9 and 39 are no longer used: the board reaches the header on pins 2 (5 V) and 6 (GND) only. EN goes to the power switch instead of staying unconnected. The signal pins below are unchanged.
5 V GND BCLK LRC DIN SD (amp on/off) Battery sense Mains sense Low-battery sense
Check continuity on GP18, GP19 and GP21 first. The Pico-to-Pi adapter's mapping for header pins 12, 35 and 40 hasn't been confirmed with a meter yet. Before soldering, beep each header pin through to the Pico GP pad named here.

The header at a glance

Pin 1 is the square pad, top left, with the board's header facing you. Odd pins run down the left column and even pins down the right. Grey pins are already taken by the e-paper HAT; leave them alone.

1 · MAX98357A amplifier and speaker

Adafruit 3006. Four signal wires to the header plus 5 V and ground, two short leads to the speaker, and a 470 µF capacitor across the amp's power pins. The firmware drives SD from GP16: low shuts the amp off completely between sounds, and high plays the left channel (the firmware sends the same mono signal on both).

The amp's pins in the order printed on the breakout: LRC, BCLK, DIN, GAIN, SD, GND, Vin. GAIN stays unconnected (9 dB). Tie it to Vin for 6 dB if the speaker is too loud at the bedside. The 470 µF capacitor sits right at the amp: its long leg (+) on Vin, the striped side (−) on GND. It covers the amp's current bursts so they don't sag the panel's supply.

2 · PowerBoost 1000C, battery and mains sensing

Adafruit 2465. It takes mains from the USB-C socket, charges the LiPo, and feeds the 5 V rail through a Schottky diode. Three sense lines come back to the Pico's analogue pins, each through a 2:1 divider, because the PowerBoost's lines reach 5 V and the Pico's analogue pins only take 3.3 V: the battery voltage, whether mains is in, and the PowerBoost's own low-battery flag.

Each divider is two 100 kΩ resistors in series from the PowerBoost pad to ground. The joint between them goes to the header pin. The 100 nF caps (dashed) are optional; the firmware already averages its readings. The dividers' grounds share header pin 39. EN stays unconnected: tying it to ground switches the whole device off.

After wiring: turn on Battery backup in Settings › Device (or the web app's Hardware card). It stays off until then because unwired analogue pins float and would show a battery that isn't there. With it on, the Hardware card shows the battery voltage and charge, so you can check the dividers straight away: a full cell should read about 4.1–4.2 V.

Before you power up

Continuity, with the power off

  1. Header pin 12 → Pico GP18, pin 35 → GP19, pin 40 → GP21, pin 36 → GP16.
  2. Pins 37, 13 and 22 → GP26, GP27 and GP28.
  3. No short between 5 V (pins 2/4) and GND.
  4. Each divider joint reads about 50 kΩ to ground.
  5. The 470 µF cap's stripe is on the amp's GND side.

First power-up

  1. Mains only, no LiPo yet: the panel should boot normally.
  2. Play rain from the web app. The amp's SD pin goes high and you should hear it within a second.
  3. Plug in the LiPo, turn on Battery backup, then pull the mains: the strip should show the battery and a popup should say "On battery".
  4. If the bench supply sags during refreshes, lower the volume first; the amp can pull up to 0.8 A.
