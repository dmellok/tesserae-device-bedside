Power and amp board

The PowerBoost and the amp sit side by side along row 10. Every line below them is one straight piece of tinned wire on the back, running down to a pin on the bottom edge, with one short insulated jumper on top where the 5 V crosses the amp's signals. The small parts stand upright between neighbouring lines. There are no solder blobs between holes, and no two joints on different lines sit side by side.

Build order

  1. Small parts, standing up. Fit R1 to R6 upright: the body stands on the hole marked with a white dot, and the top lead bends over and down into the other hole. R1, R3, R5 and R6 sit in neighbouring holes. R2 and R4 reach over one line to the next, so their bent lead spans 0.2". Fit C1 to C3 the same way: C1 and C2 span 0.2", so 5 mm legs drop straight in; C3 sits in neighbouring holes, so pinch its legs in just under the body.

  2. Diode and big cap. D1 lies flat down column M, from M9 to M5, with its band at M5. The 470 µF stands across the amp's supply in W4 and V4: long leg (+) in W4, the striped side towards V.

  3. Pin headers. Two 1×7 headers along the bottom edge: E1–K1 for the PowerBoost side, and Q1–W1 for the amp's four signals, ground and +5 V, in the amp's own pin order. Pull the pin out of T1 with pliers before fitting: GAIN stays unconnected, and the gap keys the amp's plug.

  4. The modules. Push a 1×8 header into F10–M10 and a 1×7 into Q10–W10, sit the PowerBoost and the amp on them and solder both ends. The PowerBoost's USB pad goes in F10. The amp's LRC goes in Q10 and its Vin in W10, with the speaker terminal towards the top edge. Both modules' screw terminal and battery socket face the top edge.

  5. Wires on the back, one line at a time. Each coloured line is one length of tinned wire. Lay it along its row or column, then solder it at both ends and wherever it meets a part's lead. Do the ground first: row 12 from E12 to V12, then columns E, J, L and V. Then the rest, left to right.

  6. The one jumper. A short red insulated wire on the component side, from P7 to W7, carries the 5 V over the amp's four signal lines. Strip its ends, push them through, and solder them on the back. It is the only place two lines cross.

  7. Beep test before power. On continuity, each line should beep from end to end, and no two neighbouring lines should beep together. The table below lists the neighbours worth testing.

  8. Power, USB-C only first. Plug in the USB-C, no battery, and check the voltages. Then plug in the LiPo and check again.

Where the wires go

Colours. Red is 5 V and black is ground, everywhere. The amp's lines match the gist's amp diagram: green LRC, yellow BCLK, blue DIN, and white for SD. The sense lines take green, grey and blue, picked so no two neighbouring pins on the splitter share a colour. The USB-C socket gets red and black, the switch white and black, and the speaker red to + and black to −.

PinHoleCarriesToWire

Header pin numbers as in the gist's header map. Every ground on the board is one piece, so the separate sense ground to pin 39 and the amp's ground to pin 9 are no longer needed: the black wire next to the red one carries the ground for everything. The amp takes its 5 V from this board, so splitter pin 4 is free too.

Wires to the GPIO header

Each wire in its real colour, from the board's bottom edge to the 40-pin header on the splitter, the USB-C socket and the power switch. Pin 1 is the square pad. Grey pins belong to the e-paper board: leave them alone. Wires to the right-hand column pass between the left-hand pins on their way in, so follow the colour, not the nearest pin.

Before powering up, check on continuity that header pins 12, 35 and 40 reach Pico GP18, GP19 and GP21 through the adapter, as the gist's wiring section says. Pins 4, 9 and 39 are no longer used. The switch's two wires can go either way round: they only join EN to ground when it's off.

Meter points

Set the meter to DC volts, 20 V range. The black lead stays on COM, the ground pin at V1 on the bottom edge, unless the table says otherwise. Touch the red lead on the numbered point; most are header pins you can reach from the top. Work down the table in order: each step adds one thing, so the first reading that's wrong tells you where to look.

PointHoleWhat it isExpect

A clock line reads about half of 3.3 V on DC volts, because it spends half its time high. If your meter has a Hz setting, it can read LRC directly; BCLK is above what many meters count. Point 14 is the only one with neither lead on COM. A meter floats, so it is safe across the speaker; a scope's ground clip is not.

Every sense reading stays under 3.3 V. If any of G1, H1 or K1 reads above 2.6 V, unplug and look for a missing bottom resistor.

The 470 µF is polarised. Long leg in W4, the side with the stripe towards V4. Fitted backwards it can burst.

Off means no alarm. With the switch off the Pico has no power. The e-ink keeps its last picture, so the screen won't look off.

Pad order and footprints from Adafruit's board files: PowerBoost 1000C Rev B (USB, Bat, VS, EN, GND, LBO, G, 5V) and the mono MAX98357A breakout (LRC, BCLK, DIN, GAIN, SD, GND, Vin, left to right with the speaker terminal at the top; its header is turned 180° in the file). The board size is an assumption: a common 5 × 7 cm protoboard with 24 × 18 holes; if yours is lettered the other way round, read the letters as numbers. The layout was checked by script for shorts, one piece per connection, no neighbouring joints on different lines, nothing under the modules or the 470 µF but their own pins.
