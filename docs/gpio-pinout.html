Which GPIOs are still free after the e-paper HAT

The 40-pin header you see at the GPIO Double Expansion Board is the Pi header. The adapter re-routes it onto the Pico Plus 2 W, and the HAT takes six signals plus power. Everything below is drawn from that angle: the pin you can touch on the splitter, which Pico GP it reaches, and whether anything in the firmware already owns it.

Why the two shades of "free". The vendor pinout PDF for the adapter is an unrelated ESP32-style table, so the only trustworthy mappings are the ones the firmware's pin discovery and the Spectra 6 sibling project found: BCM 8/9/10/11/15/16/17/22/24/26/27. BCM25 → GP28 was reported but not metered. Every other pin is assumed to be 1:1 (BCM n → GP n) because every verified non-SPI pin was. Put a meter between the splitter pin and the Pico header pin before soldering anything to an amber pin.

Suggested allocation for the next phases

The handover lists two things still to wire: the MAX98357A amplifier over I2S and the PowerBoost 1000C mains and battery sense lines. This plan uses the verified pins for anything analogue and the Pi header's own PCM pins for I2S, which keeps the splitter labels honest.

MAX98357A (I2S, three signals)

  • BCLK → Pi pin 12 (BCM18) → GP18
  • LRCLK → Pi pin 35 (BCM19) → GP19
  • DIN → Pi pin 40 (BCM21) → GP21
  • VIN → 5 V (pin 2 or 4), GND → any GND. Leave SD floating for L+R/2 mono, or tie it to a free pin if you want hardware mute (GP16 is a verified spare).

pico-extras audio_i2s wants BCLK and LRCLK on consecutive GPs (clock_pin_base = 18) and any pin for data; GP18/GP19/GP21 satisfy that and are the Pi's native PCM_CLK, PCM_FS and PCM_DOUT, so the splitter silkscreen matches. All three are amber: meter them first. Verified fallback if any fail: BCLK GP26, LRCLK GP27, DIN GP22, at the cost of the two ADC pins.

PowerBoost 1000C sensing

  • Bat (battery voltage) → divider → Pi pin 37 (BCM26) → GP26 / ADC0
  • USB (mains present) → divider → Pi pin 13 (BCM27) → GP27 / ADC1
  • LBO (low battery, optional) → divider → Pi pin 22 (BCM25) → GP28 / ADC2. LBO sits at battery voltage when healthy, so it needs the divider too. GP22 stays a verified spare.

GP26, GP27 and GP28 are the only ADC-capable pins the Pico Plus 2 W brings to its header (GP29 belongs to the radio), so keep the two verified ones for analogue. Bat peaks at 4.2 V and USB at 5 V; a 2:1 divider (two 100 kΩ) keeps both under the 3.3 V ADC ceiling. See the wiring guide for the divider values.

Before you solder: confirm the amber pins

With a meter (two minutes)

  1. Power off, HAT unplugged, Pico still in the adapter.
  2. Continuity from each amber splitter pin to the Pico header pin the diagram predicts (Pico pin numbers are in the circles).
  3. Also probe Pi pins 16 (BCM23) and 32 (BCM12): the diagram leaves them grey because their GP is unknown. GP0 and GP9 are the two Pico GPs with no verified Pi-side pin, so those are the likely landing spots.

With the firmware's pin discovery

The diag path in src/main.c already walks GP0 to GP29 with pulls and reports which pins move. Jumper one amber splitter pin to GND at a time, run the diag, and the GP that reads low is your answer. That is how the SPI and HRDY mappings were found in the first place.
