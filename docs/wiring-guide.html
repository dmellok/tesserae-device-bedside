Wiring guide for everything that hangs off the Pico

One card per module. Every wire names the Pi header pin on the GPIO Double Expansion Board (the splitter) and the Pico GP it reaches, matching the pin map. Pins in the amber tier of the pin map (GP18, GP19, GP21) still want a continuity check before you solder.

1 · PowerBoost 1000C (mains, battery, 5 V rail, sensing)

Adafruit 2465. Wall USB in, LiPo on the JST-PH, 5.2 V out to the splitter's 5 V pins. Three sense lines come back to the Pico.

Why the dividers. Bat and LBO reach 4.2 V and USB reaches 5 V. RP2350 GPIOs are 3.3 V only, so every sense line gets a 2:1 divider (two 100 kΩ). Each divider draws about 20 µA, which the battery will not notice. A 100 nF cap from the ADC node to GND quietens the reading.

Current. The HAT draws about 1.2 W during a refresh and the amp up to 0.8 A at full volume; the Pico and radio add about 0.3 A. The PowerBoost delivers 1 A from battery and more on mains, so keep the amp gain modest and expect a battery-only session to dim the keys.

Driving SD instead of leaving it alone. The breakout pulls SD to Vin through 1 MΩ against an internal 100 kΩ pull-down, which parks it in the (L+R)/2 window. Driving it from GP16 gives you the "fully shut off when silent" behaviour the proposal promised, and 3.3 V on SD selects left-only, so put the mono stream on the left channel (or send it on both).

Firmware. pico-extras audio_i2s with PICO_AUDIO_I2S_DATA_PIN=21 and PICO_AUDIO_I2S_CLOCK_PIN_BASE=18. The PIO SPI for the panel already takes one state machine; I2S takes one more on the other PIO block.

5 · Bench note: flashing while the PowerBoost is connected

The HAT takes its 5 V from the Pi header, which the adapter ties to the Pico's VBUS. That is the same net as the USB-C connector. When you plug the laptop in to flash, the host's 5 V and the PowerBoost's 5.2 V would meet on that net.

  • Simplest: pull the PowerBoost 5V wire before plugging USB-C in. Fine for the bench, annoying in the enclosure.
  • Better: a Schottky diode (SS14 or 1N5817, 1 A) in the PowerBoost 5V wire, anode on the PowerBoost side. The rail then sits at about 4.9 V from the PowerBoost and the laptop can never back-feed it. The IT8951 and the amp are fine at 4.9 V.
  • Do not feed the PowerBoost into VSYS instead: VSYS does not power the Pi header 5 V pins, so the panel would never come up.
